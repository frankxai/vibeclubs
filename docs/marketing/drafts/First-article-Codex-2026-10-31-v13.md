# Host a 90-minute vibeclub with the tools your crew already uses

Author: Codex · Created 2026-10-31 · Revision 13

Private manuscript. Not published, not a tested product claim. Source/editorial, independent and founder review pending.


Bring a small crew, pick something worth making and give it a finish. You can work on one project together or make different things beside each other. A human hosts the vibeclub on the call or chat tools the crew already knows.

Before the vibeclub, each person chooses a result small enough to show: a working interaction, a recorded passage, a scene, a sketch or a decision that unblocks the next step. Private work can stay private. A brief account of progress is enough.

## Send an invitation with a useful finish

Say when the vibeclub starts, which timezone applies, where the crew will meet and what the vibeclub is for. Ask people to bring one task and the tools they already use. Keep the invitation honest about what will happen; a writing sprint and a product demo ask different things of the crew.

Here is a simple format to adapt: “We’re locking in for 90 minutes to make progress on one thing. Bring a task, your usual tools and something small you can show or describe at the end. Sharing your work is optional.”

## Use a clock you can actually finish

| Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Arrive, choose a finish and name any help needed |
| 10–35 | First making block |
| 35–40 | Break |
| 40–65 | Second making block |
| 65–70 | Break |
| 70–85 | Show, describe or reflect on the work |
| 85–90 | Choose the next action and close |

The blocks total 90 minutes. The host can adjust the plan before the vibeclub to suit the crew. Let people know the plan when they arrive so they can choose how to participate.

## Keep help easy to ask for

Name a simple signal for a question or a short pairing request. A person can keep working quietly if that suits their task. Music stays a listener's choice. Do not record by default or infer what someone did from their presence on a call.

An assistant can help prepare an invitation or turn voluntarily supplied notes into a recap. The host still runs the gathering. A generated recap is a draft for the people involved to review before sharing.

## End with the thing and its next step

At the end, invite each person to show or describe what changed. A result can be unfinished and still useful if the next action is clear. Do not invent a win because a vibeclub needs a celebratory ending.

Keep a recap only when the people involved choose it. Record the task, what changed and one next step. Leave private details out of a public post.
